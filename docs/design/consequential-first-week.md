# Product strategy: useful work, a fair agreement, a different tomorrow

Proposed next milestone, 12 October 2026. Baseline: main `900015b`, runtime `4e7786c`. Explicit choices, café shifts, usable loaves, repayment and trust repairs are released. The funded library project below is **not implemented**. PR #11's neon UI is a separate draft, outside this baseline.

Read the [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md), [economic rules](economy-and-fair-contribution.md) and [implementation contracts](../implementation/community-project.md) together. Those contracts pin the prototype's records, paid units and rollout boundaries; this document owns the player experience.

## Decision

Build one small, funded community project that demonstrates **“I come back because of what I did yesterday.”** Its first session must also be satisfying: **“We changed this place together, and people remember my part.”**

The current game earns **5/10**, with an **8/10 vision**: a distinctive town and stronger reliability, but few consequential choices. These are qualitative judgments, not measured player ratings. Planned features earn no points.

Keep the five residents and the player's resident role. Kevin World's specialist think tank should emerge through people contributing different methods to a practical problem. The player participates; they do not manage an omniscient panel of autonomous experts.

## The three highest-value changes

1. **Make work useful and fairly funded.** Produce a three-record library index, pay agreed contributions from an identified budget, and independently test whether the result works.
2. **Make consequences persist visibly.** The resulting index, resident behavior, corrections and attribution must differ because of the player's choices, including after reload.
3. **Make participation easy to understand.** Show nearby actions, clear terms, progress and concise replies. Keep ordinary controls usable during ambient AI waits. Measure a fresh player's experience before adding more content.

## One project: three boxes, one usable index

Use three authored public records and three mismatched box references. Northern needs one record but cannot find it. Arwyn wants an index she can trust. Kevin proposes stable references and preserving the original labels. Sub wants an appealing public notice but risks overstating an uncertain attribution. Baba's dated delivery record supplies corroborating evidence. Personal secrets are not project rewards.

The community owns the index. Attribution gives no automatic ownership, voting power or claim on future wages.

An illustrative project budget is **8 existing coins**: café and grocery accounts each authorize 4, after protecting existing obligations. Reserve 2 each for source checking, reference testing and an independent lookup; retain 2 for a separately agreed correction task. Source checking and reference testing may share a worker; the independent lookup needs another. Each task pays once. Return all unspent project money equally after liabilities settle, including on early closure. An unresolved or underfunded prior wage claim blocks that business from funding the project.

Do not mint coins to make the example balance. Show funding, reservations, payments and refunds. Keep **money moved**, **agreed work performed** and **benefit delivered** separate. A failed experiment can fairly pay completed work without claiming a useful index. The accounting design defines the authoritative rules; dialogue only explains them.

## First session: target 10–15 minutes

At the paused 07:00 start, the guide points to Kevin's “Library project” choice and a matching public board at the library entrance. Either explains Northern's concrete difficulty without requiring a chance encounter. The player inspects the boxes and chooses whether to help. Its terms show the available contribution, fee, completion evidence and withdrawal conditions. The player can ask questions, accept, decline or walk away. Low personal wealth does not bar paid work or access to the eventual index.

Offer two contributions: check the public source cards with Arwyn, or test Kevin's references against the actual boxes. All evidence needed to find the correct boxes is available on day one; an unknown historical date does not prevent a verified current location. Each involves a short evidence-based object interaction. On completion, issue a receipt and pay the agreed fee. Advice and routine NPC contributions cannot conceal required unpaid labour: budget them or identify explicitly voluntary, bounded help.

An explicit request commissions the other funded preparation report as a written task-card response. The player combines the attributed evidence into a draft; conflicting or unsupported mappings remain provisional, and NPC reports cannot silently overwrite that choice. The player can delegate authorship to Arwyn without claiming her work. Sub then proposes the notice's wording. The player can show only verified entries, include a clearly provisional entry, or request the source check. “Test this index” books Northern's independent lookup against that submitted revision. He routes to the library for the authored appointment; a clearly previewed, optional time advance avoids waiting for a random encounter. The implementation contract protects existing commitments and defines a visible pending state for unavailable workers. **Immediate payoff:** he reaches the correct record, or visibly encounters a missing/mismatched reference and explains the problem. The notice cannot call an unverified result verified. The index and journal reflect the actual result.

Refusal creates no obligation or trust penalty; residents offer the same funded tasks to their NPC contributors, or leave the project pending if no eligible worker is available. Withdrawal preserves completed task receipts and earned payment; unstarted work is reassigned or left pending. A provisional or unsuccessful result needs correction, not wage confiscation. If work is interrupted, preserve progress without calling it complete. Do not invent partial payment terms after acceptance.

## First game week

- **Day 2:** Northern identifies the player's working entry or brings back the failed lookup. Both outcomes survive reload. Arwyn uses the verified index or visibly retains the provisional version.
- **Days 3–4:** Baba's public delivery record resolves the historical date and can support a correction of a provisional mapping or attribution; it is not a prerequisite for day-one success. Offer the reserved correction task when applicable. Sub revises the notice without requiring the player to claim authorship.
- **Days 5–7:** show the credited final revision, an explicitly provisional result, or an unfinished project with outstanding obligations. Close the account only after settling liabilities and refunding unspent funds.

These are game days. Leaving the app causes no missed-work punishment. This is one project's aftermath.

## Bread follow-on and non-goals

After the core passes its checks, prototype an optional shared meal using an explicitly offered loaf. Add a visible carrying/inventory action so eating does not require an unrelated conversation. Offer a short public gathering with Baba or Northern where the participants discuss the library result and one optional next activity. Show who accepted before consuming the loaf; one loaf can be eaten or shared once, never both. A declined invitation keeps the loaf. Memories record the meal and participants; no automatic affection bonus or essential participation gate. Eating alone remains an optional roleplay action with an honest description. No hunger meter, starvation, absence punishment or compulsory food spending. Sharing is proposed; current loaf eating does not implement it.

Defer extra residents, map expansion, broad markets, banking, token voting, procedural projects and provider migration. Account saves remain a subsequent persistence milestone. Preserve existing saves, identities and controls. A visual refresh is separate from proving this loop.

## Five-player diagnostic gate

Run five uncoached sessions, split across desktop and phone, then reopen each town on the next game day. Ask only: “Find something you want to help with, or decline it.”

Require four of five to make a chosen contribution or refusal and explain its consequence; four to recognize their own consequence after reopening; and at least three to choose a further visit without a directed reward. Ask who funded the work, who was paid, whether the result helped, and who owns it.

Record hesitations and players' words. Misunderstood causality needs clearer feedback; uninteresting consequences need better stakes. Any unintended obligation, lost earned payment, unreconciled transfer or private disclosure fails the gate. This diagnoses design, not retention or fairness at scale.
